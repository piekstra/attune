# The research behind Attune

Every recommendation in Attune carries `researchKeys` that point into this
file. This is the honest version: what each source actually found, how Attune
uses it, and where the evidence is thinner than the marketing copy of most
productivity apps would admit.

**Attune is a wellness tool, not a treatment.** Nothing here replaces
clinical care, medication, or therapy. If work and life feel unmanageable,
an ADHD-informed clinician is worth more than any app.

---

## Core mechanisms

### `reid2005` — Self-monitoring works in ADHD
Reid, R., Trout, A. L., & Schartz, M. (2005). Self-regulation interventions
for children with attention deficit/hyperactivity disorder. *Exceptional
Children, 71*(4), 361–377.

Meta-analysis of self-regulation interventions (self-monitoring,
self-management, self-reinforcement) in ADHD: combined effect sizes above
1.0 for on-task behavior and academic productivity — unusually large for any
behavioral intervention. The evidence base is strongest in children and
classrooms; the adult evidence is thinner but points the same direction.
**How Attune uses it:** the check-in *is* the intervention. Being asked
"where's your head?" a few times a day trains the noticing skill that ADHD
makes expensive. The guidance card is a bonus on top.

### `kutscheidt2019` — ADHD adults under-detect body signals
Kutscheidt, K., et al. (2019). Interoceptive awareness in patients with
attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). *ADHD Attention Deficit and
Hyperactivity Disorders, 11*, 395–401.

Adults with ADHD performed significantly worse on heartbeat-detection tasks
than controls — objective evidence of reduced interoceptive accuracy, since
corroborated by larger studies and a 2024 systematic review. Hunger, thirst,
fatigue, and rising stress are exactly the signals that arrive late or not
at all. **How Attune uses it:** the check-in asks about the body separately
from the mind ("And your body?"), and body-care guidance treats missed meals
and hydration as an expected sensor gap, not a personal failing.

### `hupfeld2019` — Hyperfocus is real, common, and double-edged
Hupfeld, K. E., Abagis, T. R., & Shah, P. (2019). Living "in the zone":
hyperfocus in adult ADHD. *ADHD Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity
Disorders, 11*, 191–208.

Across two samples (n=251, n=372), adults with higher ADHD symptomatology
reported more frequent and more intense hyperfocus across settings. Long
unbroken high-focus stretches routinely override eating, drinking, and
movement. **How Attune uses it:** the hyperfocus guard — consecutive
high-focus check-ins with no break for 100+ minutes triggers body-care
guidance that protects the streak while tending the body, instead of
breaking the flow.

### `ptacek2019` / `barkley1997` — Time blindness
Ptáček, R., et al. (2019). Clinical implications of the perception of time
in attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD): a review. *Medical
Science Monitor, 25*, 3918–3924.
Barkley, R. A. (1997). Behavioral inhibition, sustained attention, and
executive functions: constructing a unifying theory of ADHD. *Psychological
Bulletin, 121*(1), 65–94.

Time perception and time reproduction are reliably altered in ADHD; Barkley's
influential model treats degraded self-directed timing as a core deficit and
prescribes *externalizing time* as the compensation. **How Attune uses it:**
timers everywhere (break timers with a return prompt, sprint timers), and the
activity monitor that answers "how long did I actually work today?" with a
measured number instead of a dread-shaped guess. The memory of a scattered
day compresses toward "I barely worked"; the counter is the corrective
evidence, which matters because the usual response to that feeling is
anxious overtime.

## Breaks and recovery

### `albulescu2022` — Micro-breaks measurably help
Albulescu, P., et al. (2022). "Give me a break!" A systematic review and
meta-analysis on the efficacy of micro-breaks for increasing well-being and
performance. *PLOS ONE, 17*(8), e0272460.

Meta-analysis (22 studies): micro-breaks produce small but significant gains
in vigor (d=.36) and reductions in fatigue (d=.35). Performance recovery
after cognitively demanding work needs longer breaks (10+ minutes). **How
Attune uses it:** short breaks are the default prescription for mid-focus
dips; genuinely depleted states get longer restorative breaks, because the
evidence says short ones won't cut it there.

### `kaplan1995` / `berman2008` — Attention restoration
Kaplan, S. (1995). The restorative benefits of nature: toward an integrative
framework. *Journal of Environmental Psychology, 15*(3), 169–182.
Berman, M. G., Jonides, J., & Kaplan, S. (2008). The cognitive benefits of
interacting with nature. *Psychological Science, 19*(12), 1207–1212.

Directed attention is depletable; natural environments restore it measurably
(Berman: ~20% working-memory span improvement after a nature walk vs a city
walk). Feeds and inboxes keep spending it. **How Attune uses it:** depleted
states get "outside, near anything green" — and never "take a break on your
phone."

### `mehren2019` / `denheijer2017` — Acute exercise sharpens ADHD attention
Mehren, A., et al. (2019). Acute effects of aerobic exercise on executive
function and attention in adult patients with ADHD. *Frontiers in
Psychiatry, 10*, 132.
Den Heijer, A. E., et al. (2017). Sweat it out? The effects of physical
exercise on cognition and behavior in children and adults with ADHD.
*Journal of Neural Transmission, 124*, 3–26.

A single bout of moderate aerobic exercise improved reaction times on
attention tasks in adults with ADHD (but not controls) — one of the most
reliable non-medication effects in the literature, acting on the same
catecholamine systems stimulant medication targets. **How Attune uses it:**
"wired/restless" energy always routes to movement; several low-focus states
offer short heart-rate-raising breaks before anything else.

## Interruptions and task switching

### `mark2008` — Interruptions cost speed and stress
Mark, G., Gudith, D., & Klocke, U. (2008). The cost of interrupted work:
more speed and stress. *Proceedings of CHI 2008*, 107–110.

Interrupted workers compensate by working faster at the price of higher
stress, frustration, and effort. **How Attune uses it:** high focus is never
interrupted with advice — the app says "I'll get out of the way" and
lengthens its own interval. The check-in cadence backs off when things work.

### `iqbal2010` — Deliver interruptions at boundaries
Iqbal, S. T., & Bailey, B. P. (2010). Oasis: A framework for linking
notification delivery to the perceptual structure of goal-directed tasks.
*ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 17*(4), 1–28.

Notifications delivered at task boundaries (the coarser the better) carry
measurably lower resumption cost, frustration, and error rates than
notifications delivered mid-task. **How Attune uses it:** check-ins never
fire during a detected meeting; they arrive a couple of minutes *after* the
call ends — the coarsest, cheapest boundary of the knowledge-work day.

### `leroy2009` — Attention residue
Leroy, S. (2009). Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of
attention residue when switching between work tasks. *Organizational
Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 109*(2), 168–181.

After a task switch, part of attention stays with the unfinished task and
degrades performance on the next one; a structured "ready-to-resume" moment
reduces the residue. **How Attune uses it:** every context switch and break
carries a return ritual — write where you stopped, name the first move back.
The "welcome back" prompt at timer end re-delivers exactly that.

### `gazzaley2016` — Environments beat willpower
Gazzaley, A., & Rosen, L. D. (2016). *The Distracted Mind: Ancient Brains in
a High-Tech World.* MIT Press.

A neuroscientist's synthesis of interference research: goal-directed
attention loses to engineered cues, and the reliable countermeasure is
changing the environment (fewer visible cues) rather than resisting harder.
**How Attune uses it:** the environment fixes — close the tabs, Do Not
Disturb, phone in another room — are framed as one-time setup moves, never
as tests of resolve.

### `masicampo2011` — Written plans release open loops
Masicampo, E. J., & Baumeister, R. F. (2011). Consider it done! Plan making
can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals. *Journal of
Personality and Social Psychology, 101*(4), 667–683.

Unfinished goals intrude on working memory (the Zeigarnik effect); writing a
*specific plan* for them stops the intrusions even before the goal is done.
**How Attune uses it:** the worry parking lot and the end-of-day "write
tomorrow's first move" both convert circling loops into stored plans.

## Motivation and follow-through

### `gawrilow2008` / `gollwitzer1999` — If-then plans work in ADHD
Gawrilow, C., & Gollwitzer, P. M. (2008). Implementation intentions
facilitate response inhibition in children with ADHD. *Cognitive Therapy and
Research, 32*, 261–280.
Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999). Implementation intentions: strong effects of
simple plans. *American Psychologist, 54*(7), 493–503.

Implementation intentions ("when X happens, I will Y") brought response
inhibition in children with ADHD up to non-ADHD levels in Go/NoGo tasks —
notable because if-then plans delegate control to the situation cue,
bypassing exactly the self-initiated executive control ADHD taxes.
**How Attune uses it:** every redirect ends with an if-then return plan
("When the timer rings, I sit down and open ___"), phrased as words the user
says, not settings they configure.

### `sonugabarke2003` / `dodson-inb` — Interest-driven motivation
Sonuga-Barke, E. J. S. (2003). The dual pathway model of AD/HD: an
elaboration of neuro-developmental characteristics. *Neuroscience &
Biobehavioral Reviews, 27*(7), 593–604.
Dodson, W. (clinical writings on the "interest-based nervous system").

Sonuga-Barke's dual-pathway model identifies motivational/delay-aversion
circuitry as a core pathway in ADHD, separate from executive deficits.
Dodson's "interest-based nervous system" framing — ADHD engagement runs on
interest, novelty, challenge, and urgency rather than importance — is
clinical observation, widely resonant but not itself an RCT; we label it
accordingly. **How Attune uses it:** boredom routes to the sprint game
(manufactured urgency and finish lines), and recommendation copy rotates
variants because novelty itself is fuel here.

### `eagle-bodydoubling` — Body doubling
Eagle, T., & Ringland, K. E. (2023). Studies of "body doubling" practices in
ADHD communities (CSCW/ASSETS work on technology-mediated body doubling).

Body doubling — working alongside another person to borrow their presence as
structure — is one of the most widely used community-developed ADHD
strategies. Formal effectiveness research is early; the practice earns its
place by ubiquity and low cost, and we say so. **How Attune uses it:** one
environment-fix suggestion offers it as an option.

## Rhythms and stopping

### `coogan2017` — ADHD chronotypes run late and vary
Coogan, A. N., & McGowan, N. M. (2017). A systematic review of circadian
function, chronotype and chronotherapy in attention deficit hyperactivity
disorder. *ADHD Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity Disorders, 9*, 129–147.

Delayed sleep phase is dramatically over-represented in ADHD (self-reported
prevalence ~26% vs ≤3% general population); melatonin onset runs ~90 minutes
late on average. Peak cognitive hours vary more, and run later, than
standard office rhythm assumes. **How Attune uses it:** the Insights window
learns *your* sharpest window from your own check-ins instead of assuming
9-to-5, and suggests guarding it for deep work.

### `warm2008` — Vigilance is hard work
Warm, J. S., Parasuraman, R., & Matthews, G. (2008). Vigilance requires hard
mental work and is stressful. *Human Factors, 50*(3), 433–441.

Sustained attention measurably declines with time on task for everyone —
the vigilance decrement — and the decline is effortful and stressful, not
lazy. **How Attune uses it:** late-day low focus is treated as physiology,
and the response is a structured landing, not a pep talk.

### `sonnentag2007` — Detaching from work is what restores you
Sonnentag, S., & Fritz, C. (2007). The Recovery Experience Questionnaire:
development and validation of a measure for assessing recuperation and
unwinding from work. *Journal of Occupational Health Psychology, 12*(3),
204–221.

Establishes *psychological detachment* — mentally switching off from work,
not merely being off the clock — as a distinct recovery experience that
predicts wellbeing, lower exhaustion, and better next-day functioning. Days
that never bracket shut (early check-ins, after-hours "quick looks") erode
exactly this. **How Attune uses it:** when the full-day *span* — first
activity to last, breaks included — stretches long while focus fades, the
guidance shifts from "did you do enough?" to "the win now is separation."
Attune measures the bracket the user can't feel, and treats a real evening
as a legitimate goal rather than an earned reward.

### `borkovec1983` / `zaccaro2018` — Worry postponement and slow breathing
Borkovec, T. D., Wilkinson, L., Folensbee, R., & Lerman, C. (1983). Stimulus
control applications to the treatment of worry. *Behaviour Research and
Therapy, 21*(3), 247–251.
Zaccaro, A., et al. (2018). How breath-control can change your life: a
systematic review on psycho-physiological correlates of slow breathing.
*Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 12*, 353.

Scheduling a specific later time to worry reliably reduces intrusive worry
(the mind releases a loop it trusts will be revisited); slow breathing with
extended exhales shifts autonomic balance toward the parasympathetic.
**How Attune uses it:** the worry tools — parking lot with a named
revisit time, and 4-in/6-out breathing before deciding anything.

## Honesty section

Things a careful reader should know:

- **The check-in evidence is strongest in children and classrooms.** Adult
  workplace EMA/self-monitoring studies exist and point the same way, but
  the effect sizes above come from the child literature.
- **Nobody has run an RCT on this app's exact combination** of adaptive
  cadence, meeting deferral, and legitimized context switching. The design
  composes mechanisms that are individually supported; the composition is
  hypothesis, not proof.
- **Activity tracking measures presence, not productivity.** That is a
  feature (see DESIGN.md), but don't mistake the number for output.
- **The glucose/willpower literature was deliberately left out** of the
  "eat something" advice — it failed replication. Eating when you haven't
  eaten needs no fancier justification than being a mammal.
- **Dodson's interest-based nervous system** is clinical framing, not a
  validated construct; it appears here because it describes a pattern many
  ADHD adults recognize instantly, clearly labeled as what it is.
